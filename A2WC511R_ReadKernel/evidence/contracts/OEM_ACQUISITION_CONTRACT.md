The complete fixed upload is executed through the actual `1440` RDR parser, `34/36/31` dispatch, `1E3C` decryption, `1646` stores, low16 checksum, TDR acknowledgement producer and `1C28` handoff. Seven negative controls reject invalid declarations, wrong start subcommands and a bad upload sum.

One negative control is particularly important: the OEM decrypts and writes RAM during `36`, before its trailing packet checksum is checked. A bad checksum can leave RAM changed and produces no positive acknowledgement. The host must stop on that failure. This is temporary RAM modification, not flash writing; the additive checksum and `5AA5` sum do not authenticate arbitrary corruption.

Ordinary tasks before the programming transition are not subjected to an impossible whole-firmware no-write claim. The reviewed path begins at the actual admitted KWP/interrupt state and follows its bounded callees. The installed vehicle's acquisition code must be stock-compatible, RAM/upload must remain intact, and no unexpected DMA, nested interrupt, NMI, reset or power event may divert the path. The loader reaches I15 before upload, while the owned kernel subsequently installs its own SP/VBR, disables DMA/WDT and initializes private RAM. Physical clock, UART/adapter, external watchdog, pin effects and verified power-loss exit remain separate gates.
